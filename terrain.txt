Soil & Granular Surface Composition

    Quarry Fines & Silt Dust: Fine-grained, pulverized soil designed to emulate loose Martian regolith. It offers low shear strength, causing wheel sinkage, high rolling resistance, and dust kick-up near drive motors.

    Loose Gravel & Scree Beds: Mixed aggregate layers consisting of 10 mm to 50 mm loose stones. Traversing these causes lateral tire slip, loss of odometry accuracy, and wheel spinning during acceleration.

    Compacted Clay & Hardpack: Stabilized dirt paths situated near starting grids and servicing panels, providing high traction before suddenly transitioning into soft, loose soil.

Topographical Formations & Elevation Profiles

    Artificial Craters: Depressions ranging from 0.3 m to over 1.5 m in depth. Entering and exiting requires high motor torque and anti-roll stability to prevent tip-overs.

    Mounds & Ridges: Earthwork hills featuring slopes from 15° to over 30°. These test passive chassis articulation (e.g., Rocker-Bogie or Differential Swivel mechanisms) to ensure all wheels remain in contact with the ground.

    Rifts & Gullies: Steep-walled trenches cutting across navigation paths. These act as chassis traps and obscure line-of-sight for obstacle-detection sensors.

Obstacle Density & Hazards

    Boulder Fields: Unstructured distributions of rocks ranging from small cobbles (5–10 cm) to large boulders (30–60 cm).

    Ground Clearance Traps: Underbody clearance of at least 20–25 cm is required to prevent the central electronics belly pan or differential linkage from high-centering on rocks.

    Blind Drops: Abrupt drop-offs behind crests that require dynamic local path planning to prevent front-over rollovers.
